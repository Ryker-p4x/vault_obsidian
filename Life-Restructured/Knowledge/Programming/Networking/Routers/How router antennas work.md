Your doughnut analogy is spot-on for standard router antennas, but the oval description can be refined further depending on the antenna type.

### Radiation Patterns

The 3D area where an antenna sends its signal is called its **radiation pattern**:

- **Omnidirectional Antennas (The Doughnut Shape):**
    
    - **How it works:** Most home routers use stick-like antennas (dipole antennas). They radiate signal outwards in a 360-degree circle perpendicular to the antenna stick, with dead zones directly above the tip and below the base.
        
    - **In simple terms:** If the antenna is pointing straight up, the signal spreads out far along the horizontal floor (the thick part of the doughnut), but goes very little up toward the ceiling or down toward the floor (the hole of the doughnut).
        
- **Directional Antennas (The Flashlight / Cone Shape):**
    
    - **How it works:** Instead of an oval, think of a flashlight beam or a tear-drop cone shape.
        
    - **In simple terms:** These push almost all their energy forward in one specific direction. The signal reaches much farther in that one direction, but provides almost zero signal behind or to the sides of the antenna.
        

### Higher Gain (The Flattened Doughnut)

When an omnidirectional antenna is described as "stronger" (higher gain), it doesn't just grow overall like a balloon. Instead, **the doughnut gets squished flatter**.

- **Result:** The signal pushes out even _farther_ horizontally, but the coverage above and below it shrinks even more.
    

### Why Routers Have Multiple Antennas

Multiple antennas aren't just for sending the signal further in one direction; they serve three primary functions:

1. **Multiple Streams (MIMO / Spatial Multiplexing):** Modern routers use **MIMO** (Multiple-Input Multiple-Output). Having multiple antennas allows the router to talk to a single device (or multiple devices) using separate data lanes simultaneously, dramatically multiplying speeds.
    
2. **Beamforming (Focusing the Signal):** Routers with multiple antennas can coordinate their signals so that waves overlap and construct a stronger beam aimed directly at your connected device (like a phone or laptop), rather than just blasting the signal everywhere blindly.
    
3. **Polarization Diversity:** A laptop on a table usually has horizontal internal antennas, while a smartphone held upright has vertical ones. Positioning multiple router antennas at different angles (e.g., one straight up, one at a 45-degree angle) ensures the router matches the orientation of your devices.
    

### Quick Positioning Tips

- **Single-Floor Home:** Keep antennas pointing **straight up**. This sends the widest "doughnut" of signal across your entire floor.
    
- **Multi-Floor Home:** Angle one or two antennas to a **45-degree angle** (or point one horizontally). This tilts the "doughnut" pattern so signal can reach upstairs and downstairs rooms.
