	## 3-Day Calisthenics Program with Tendon Work

**Schedule:** Monday, Wednesday and Friday, alternating A and B (A, B, A one week, then B, A, B the next). Start from week 3 of your current plan.

### Warm-up (5 min, every day)

- Arm circles, wrist rolls and finger stretches: 30 sec each
- Bodyweight squats: 10
- Calf raises: 10
- 1-2 easy reps of your first exercise

### Tempo rule (applies to every exercise below)

Lower for **3 seconds** and pause **1 second** in the stretched or bottom position, then press or lift normally. It costs nothing extra and loads tendons more than fast reps.

### What I added beyond the tendon list

- **Backpack hold by the handle:** a heavy bag hanging from one hand trains finger flexors and the forearm tendons you use in every pull and row.
- **Chair step-ups (optional, Day A, instead of the second set of lunges):** 2 sets of 8 per leg, slow lowering. They load the patellar tendon well. Use a sturdy chair against a wall.
- **Hamstring walkouts (optional, Day B):** lie on your back, feet on the floor, lift your hips and slowly walk your feet out and back. 2 sets of 6-8. They strengthen the hamstring tendons that glute bridges only partly cover.

Add these only after week 3 of the tendon work, one at a time.

### Phasing the tendon work

|Weeks|What to do|
|---|---|
|1-3|Tempo on every exercise, calf raises on both days, one hold at the end of each day|
|4-6|Add the remaining holds, tibialis raises and wrist work|
|7+|Add the optional moves, then progress holds and load slowly|

### Progression rules

- **Reps:** hit the top of the rep range on all sets, then make it harder. Push-ups go from knee to standard to decline, lunges and bridges get backpack weight, and rows get more books in the bag.
- **Holds and tendon work:** add about 5 sec or one rep every 1-2 weeks. Tendons need weeks to adapt, not days.
- **Effort:** stop 1-2 reps before failure on most sets, and go close to failure on the last set of each exercise. Keep holds at about 6-7 out of 10 effort.
- **Rest:** 2-3 minutes after the big moves (lunges, push-ups, rows, bridges), and 60-90 seconds after arms, raises, core and tendon work.
- **Log everything:** reps, hold times and backpack weight. Beating last week by one rep or a few seconds counts.

### Notes

- **Pain rule:** muscle soreness is fine. Sharp or lingering pain in an elbow, knee, wrist or Achilles means you should cut the load or the hold time for several days. Don’t push through it.
- **Fatigue check:** if you feel run down, cut the small exercises (raises, curls, crunches) to 2 sets before touching the main lifts or the tendon block. Tendon work gets priority over extra accessory volume.
- **Pull-ups** are optional. Only add them if you have a bar or a truly sturdy spot, and start with slow lowering reps. Until then, table rows and backpack rows cover your back.
- **Food and sleep:** a calorie surplus, enough protein and 7-9 hours of sleep still drive the results, and tendons recover more slowly than muscle.
